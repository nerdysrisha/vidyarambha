27. Presente do indicativo: regulares e irregulares (Present tense — full reference)

The presente do indicativo is the everyday present tense. It describes habits (Eu faço o almoço todos os dias), facts, what is happening now and plans for the near future (Falamos amanhã). Portuguese has no separate "-ing" form, so falo means both "I speak" and "I am speaking".

Every verb has an infinitive ending in -ar, -er or -ir (the 1st, 2nd and 3rd conjugation). For regular verbs you remove that ending to get the root and add the endings below: estudar → estud + o = estudo. Irregular verbs change their root or endings (fazer → faço, ser → sou) and must be learnt by heart.

Remember: tu is informal; você uses the same form as ele / ela; vocês uses the same form as eles / elas. The subject pronoun is often dropped (Falo português), não goes before the verb (Não bebem leite) and a question is made with rising intonation or a question word.

These four verbs are used all the time. Their eu forms are irregular (faço, digo, posso, quero), and faz, diz, quer are shorter than you would expect. The tu, nós, vocês / eles forms follow the normal -er endings.

Verbos irregulares muito utilizados — exemplos

Eu faço o almoço todos os dias.I make lunch every day.

Tu dizes a verdade.You tell the truth.

Nós podemos falar português.We can speak Portuguese.

Eles querem aprender português.They want to learn Portuguese.

A model self-introduction, exactly as written by your professor. The verb in brackets is the infinitive of each verb used.

Falo (falar) português um pouco, mas falo muito bem punjabi, hindi e inglês. Moro (morar) em Saldanha, na rua Praia de Vitória, número (=n°) 6, terceiro (=3°) andar, direito (d°). Use this as a template for your own introduction: greeting, name, origin, languages, address, work, age, marital status, family and hobbies. The verbs are mostly present-tense regulars (falo, moro, trabalho, vivem, gosto) plus ser (sou) and ter (tenho).
