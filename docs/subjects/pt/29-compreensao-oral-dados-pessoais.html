Compreensão oral — Dados pessoais (Listening comprehension — Personal data)

In this lesson you listen and then write. Christos is a new student at a language school in Lisbon. He talks to the school secretary (a secretária), who asks for his personal data (dados pessoais) and fills in his ficha (registration form). Your task is the same as hers: listen to the audio and complete the form. The exercise comes from Passaporte para Português, Aud-33, p. 38. The instruction on the worksheet reads: Ouça a conversa com a secretária da escola e complete a ficha. (Listen to the conversation with the school secretary and complete the form.)

Every question can be asked in two ways. With a stranger, an older person or in an office, use the formal form: Como se chama? Qual é o seu nome? With a friend or a young person, use tu: Como te chamas? Qual é o teu nome? In Portugal the formal form often drops the pronoun, so you say Como se chama? rather than Como é que você se chama? Three small points matter for your answers: (1) age uses ter (to have): Tenho 32 anos (literally "I have 32 years"), never sou; (2) nationality, marital status and profession change with gender: grego/grega, solteiro/solteira, professor/professora; (3) profession takes no article: Sou professor, not Sou um professor.

2. Morada, números e símbolos (address, numbers and symbols)

An address is written Rua, nº, andar, lado, for example Av. da Liberdade, nº 25, 2º Esq., read as número vinte e cinco, segundo esquerdo. R/C is the ground floor: R/C Dto. is read rés-do-chão direito. The postal code has four digits, a hyphen and three digits (1200-183). Phone numbers have nine digits; Lisbon landlines start with 21 and mobile numbers with 9. People read them in single digits or small groups. An e-mail such as ana.costa@mail.pt is read ana ponto costa arroba mail ponto pt.

  5. Do you have a landline? Tem telefone (fixo)?
