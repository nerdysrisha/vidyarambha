Imperativo: verbos regulares (giving commands & requests — आज्ञा / अनुरोध)

The imperative (o imperativo) is the verb form we use to give an order, an instruction or a request: "Sing!", "Open the door!", "Don't touch!" In Hindi this is the आज्ञार्थ (आदेश / अनुरोध) form: गाओ! दरवाज़ा खोलो! मत छुओ! It is not rude by itself. Add por favor (please) and it becomes a polite request: Canta, por favor! गाओ, प्लीज़!

Hindi already has "levels" of politeness, and Portuguese does too: tu (informal, like तुम / तू) → canta! (गाओ); você (formal, like आप) → cante! (गाइए); vocês (plural, "all of you") → cantem! (आप सब गाइए). The Portuguese verb changes with who you speak to, and also with yes or no: canta (sing!) vs não cantes (don't sing!), just like गाओ vs मत गाओ.

Four points to notice. (1) The imperative has no "eu": you cannot command yourself. The persons are tu, você, nós ("let's…") and vocês; ele / ela use the você form and eles / elas use the vocês form. (2) Affirmative tu copies the present-tense ele / ela form: ele fala → fala! (3) Every other form (and all negatives) uses the "opposite vowel": -ar verbs switch to e, -er / -ir verbs switch to a. (4) Politeness words soften the order: por favor, faz favor, se faz favor. In writing they are shortened to pf and sff.

Truque: affirmative tu = ele / ela of the present (ele fala → fala!). Negative tu = present tu with the vowel switched (falas → não fales). Ele / ela / você / o senhor / a senhora all use the same você form.

Nós: in everyday speech Portuguese people usually say Vamos falar! (चलो बोलते हैं!) instead of falemos.

Conversas — pedidos e respostas

1. Em casa — tu (informal)

Ana: Abre a janela, por favor.Open the window, please.खिड़की खोलो, प्लीज़।

Rui: Claro, abro já.Sure, I'll open it right away.ज़रूर, अभी खोलता हूँ।
