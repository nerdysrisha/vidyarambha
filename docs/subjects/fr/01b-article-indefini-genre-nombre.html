1b. Article indéfini, genre, nombre, démonstratifs, couleurs et possession (Indefinite article, gender, number, invariable demonstratives, colors, and possession)

Following on from Lesson 1 (Articles): that lesson introduced le/la/les (definite) and un/une/des (indefinite). This lesson goes deeper — it explains why a noun takes un or une (gender), how nouns change in the plural, and then introduces three related topics: demonstratives, colors, and possession.

  • Noun gender and the indefinite article — un and une
  • Plural formation — how French nouns change from singular to plural
  • Demonstratives — ceci, cela, and ça
  • Colors — how colors are used and agree with nouns
  • Possession — using de (and its contractions du/des) to say something belongs to someone

Article indéfini et genre des noms (Indefinite article and noun gender)

Masculin (Masculine) Féminin (Feminine)
Article (Article) un une
Terminaisons courantes (Common endings) -age, -ment, -eau (le voyage, le moment, le bureau)
most consonant endings		 -tion, -sion, -té (la nation, la beauté)
-ette, -ance, -ence (most abstract nouns)
Exception (Exception) A number of common nouns end in -e but are masculine — you just have to memorize them: le problème, le système, le programme, le silence, le groupe

Attention : nouns ending in -e don't follow a reliable rule for gender either way — you need to learn each one individually. Compare le stage (the internship) with la page (the page), or le groupe (the group) with la chose (the thing).

Note : le livre → les livres keeps the definite article in the plural — same word for both genders. For the indefinite article: un livre → des livres ("a book" → "some books"). Unlike Portuguese, French almost always keeps an article even in general statements: J'aime les livres ("I like books") uses les, never no article at all.

Démonstratifs invariables (Invariable demonstratives)

Français English
cecithis (near the speaker — more formal/written)
celathat (neutral, standard register)
çathat / this (informal — by far the most common in everyday speech)

Note : unlike Portuguese, which has a three-way distance system (isto/isso/aquilo — near me / near you / far from both), French mostly collapses this to cela or, in everyday speech, simply ça — used for almost everything regardless of distance. Ceci exists but is comparatively rare outside careful writing.

Couleurs (Colors)

Français English
blanc / blanche / blancs / blancheswhite (agrees in gender and number)
vert / verte / verts / vertesgreen (agrees in gender and number)
bleu / bleue / bleus / bleuesblue (agrees in gender and number)
marronbrown (fully invariable — never changes)

Attention : unlike Portuguese, where verde and azul stay the same for masculine and feminine, French vert/verte and bleu/bleue do change for gender. Marron, on the other hand, never changes at all — same pattern as Portuguese's invariable cor de rosa.

Possession avec de (Possession with "of")

Français English
de + le = duof the (masculine)
de + les = desof the (plural)
de + la = de laof the (feminine — no contraction)
de + l' = de l'of the (before a vowel sound — no contraction)

Note : de only contracts with le and les — never with la or l'. le stylo du professeur ("the teacher's pen"). It also stays uncontracted before a name with no article, like a person's name: un tableau de Picasso, une carte de France.

Règles rapides (Quick rules)

• Adjectives (colors, nationalities) usually come after the noun: Le drapeau bleu et blanc est de la Grèce.

• We use un/une when talking about something indefinite, or mentioning it for the first time.

• We use le/la when talking about something definite, or something already known.

Exemples

Article indéfini

J'ai un nouveau livre.I have a new book. (first mention)

C'est une grande fenêtre.It's a big window.

Pluriel des noms

Les livres sont sur la table.The books are on the table.

Il y a deux chiens dans le jardin.There are two dogs in the garden.

Démonstratifs invariables

Ceci est un ordinateur.This is a computer. (near me)

C'est à toi, ça ?Is that yours?

Cela, c'est la tour.That is the tower. (further away)

Couleurs

La porte est noire.The door is black.

Les livres sont bleus.The books are blue.

Le drapeau bleu et blanc est de la Grèce.The blue and white flag is Greece's. (adjectives after the noun)

Possession avec de

C'est le dictionnaire de Jean.It's Jean's dictionary.

L'ordinateur du professeur est un Toshiba.The teacher's computer is a Toshiba.

Exercices — Article indéfini, nombre, démonstratifs, couleurs et possession

A. Complétez avec un ou une.

1. une fenêtre (exemple)
2. ___un dictionnaire
3. ___une carte
4. ___un mur
5. ___une horloge
6. ___un journal
7. ___une table
8. ___un ordinateur

Note: carte (map) is feminine in French — the opposite gender from Portuguese's o mapa, which is masculine. Gender doesn't always match across related languages.

1. l'ordinateur → les ordinateurs (exemple)
2. une horloge → ___________des horloges

5. le pays → ___________les pays

D. Complétez avec de, de la, ou du.

Proper names don't take an article, so de stays uncontracted: un tableau de Picasso.

3. Le bureau brun est ___de Claire.
4. L'horloge ___de Julie est blanche.
5. La chaise ___du directeur est brune.
6. C'est une carte ___de France.
