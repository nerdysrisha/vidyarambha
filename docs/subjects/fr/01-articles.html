1. Articles (Articles)

Articles come before a noun and agree with its gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural). A definite article ("the") points to something specific or already known — la voiture means "the car" (a particular one you both know). An indefinite article ("a/an", "some") introduces something unspecified or new — une voiture means "a car" (any car, not a specific one). Unlike Portuguese, French drops the gender distinction in the plural: les and des work for both masculine and feminine.

Défini (Definite) Indéfini (Indefinite)
le (the, masc.) (वह) un (a/an, masc.) (एक)
la (the, fem.) (वह) une (a/an, fem.) (एक)
les (the, pl.) (वे) des (some, pl.) (कुछ)

Basic idea: the article changes according to gender in the singular, but the plural is the same for both genders. Also note: le/la shorten to l' before a vowel sound — l'ami, l'école.
