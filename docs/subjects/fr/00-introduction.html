Personal identification and description

Je m'appelle Jean Dubois.My name is Jean Dubois.

Je viens d'Angleterre, je suis de Londres.I'm from England, I'm from London.

Je parle un peu français, je parle très bien anglais.I speak a little French, I speak English very well.

J'habite à Saldanha.I live in Saldanha.

Je travaille dans l'agriculture / Je travaille dans un restaurant.I work in agriculture / I work in a restaurant.

J'ai 30 ans. Je ne suis pas marié.I am 30 years old. I am not married.

Mes parents vivent en Inde.My parents live in India.

J'aime beaucoup regarder le football. J'aime écouter de la musique indienne.I like watching football a lot. I like listening to Indian music.

Les Personnes (The Persons)

Every French sentence starts from a person — who is speaking, who is being spoken to, or who is being spoken about. In the introduction above, Jean only used the je ("I") form. As you go on, you'll meet the other persons below, and each one changes how the verb is conjugated. Note that French only distinguishes formal/informal in the singular — vous covers both "you" (formal, singular) and "you all" (plural, any register).

Français English Hindi Notes
je I मैं 1st person, singular
tu you (informal) तू / तुम 2nd person, singular, informal — like Hindi तुम
vous you (formal, singular) आप 2nd person, singular, formal — like Hindi आप
il / elle he / she वह 3rd person, singular
nous we हम 1st person, plural
vous you all तुम सब / आप लोग 2nd person, plural — same word as formal "you" above, context tells them apart
ils / elles they वे 3rd person, plural

Basic idea: the verb ending changes depending on which person is doing the action — that's what the following pages will drill.
