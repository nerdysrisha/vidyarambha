Chapter 15 — Purushottama Yoga

The Yoga of the Supreme Divine Personality · 20 verses
ఓం శ్రీ గురుభ్యో నమః । ఓం శ్రీ పరమాత్మనే నమః । అథ శ్రీమద్భగవద్గీతా । అథ పంచదశోఽధ్యాయః (పురుషోత్తమయోగః) ।।
తా॥ ఓం. గురువులకు నమస్కారం. పరమాత్మకు నమస్కారం. ఇప్పుడు శ్రీమద్భగవద్గీత కొనసాగుతోంది. ఇది పదిహేనవ అధ్యాయం; దీని పేరు “పురుషోత్తమయోగం”. ఈ అధ్యాయంలో భгవంతుడు సంసారాన్ని ఒక తలక్రిందులుగా ఉన్న అశ్వత్థ (రావి) వృక్షంతో పోలుస్తూ, దానిని వైరాగ్యమనే ఆయుధంతో ఎలా ఛేదించాలో వివరిస్తాడు. క్షరుడు (నశించే శరీరం), అక్షరుడు (నశించని ఆత్మ) కంటే ఉత్తముడైన ఆ పరమాత్మ తానేనని, అందుకే తనకు 'పురుషోత్తముడు' అనే పేరు వచ్చిందని శ్రీకృష్ణుడు తెలియజేస్తాడు.
om śrī gurubhyo namaḥ | om śrī paramātmane namaḥ | atha śrīmadbhagavadgītā | atha pañcadaśo 'dhyāyaḥ (puruṣottama-yogaḥ)
Om. Salutations to the teachers. Salutations to the Supreme Being. Now the Shrimad Bhagavad Gita continues. This is the fifteenth chapter, called "Purushottama Yoga." In this chapter, Sri Krishna compares worldly existence to an upside-down ashvattha tree and tells how to cut it down with the weapon of non-attachment. He shows the difference between the perishable, the imperishable, and the individual soul, and declares that the Supreme Being, higher than all of them, is himself, which is why he is called Purushottama.

· · · ✦ · · ·

The Lord, who at the end of the fourteenth chapter said that undivided devotion is the way to cross the qualities and that he is the abode of Brahman, the immortal, and eternal dharma, here expands on what that abode is like and how to reach it.

· · · ✦ · · ·

Purushottama Yoga — the yoga of knowing and reaching the Supreme Being, the best of all persons; the Purushottama whom Arjuna addressed in the eighth chapter here himself gives the reason for that name.
ఊర్ధ్వమూలమధః శాఖమశ్వత్థం ప్రాహురవ్యయమ్ ।
ఛందాంసి యస్య పర్ణాని యస్తం వేద స వేదవిత్ ।। 1 ।।
శ్రీ భగవానుడు పలికెను : వేర్లు పైకి మరియు కొమ్మలు క్రిందికి ఉన్న సనాతనమైన అశ్వత్థ వృక్షము గురించి చెప్తుంటారు. దాని యొక్క ఆకులు వేద మంత్రములు, మరియు ఈ చెట్టు యొక్క రహస్యం తెలిసిన వారు వేదములను తెలుసుకున్నట్టు.
sri-bhagavan uvaca
urdhva-mulam adhah-sakham
asvattham prahur avyayam
chandamsi yasya parnani
yas tam veda sa veda-vit
The Blessed Lord said: "They speak of an imperishable ashvattha tree with its roots above and branches below; its leaves are the Vedic meters; one who knows it is a knower of the Veda." This is a picture given of worldly existence: the Supreme Being, the source of creation, is above, and the world that spread out of it extends downward as branches. A tree that looks upside down suggests that the world is a reflection that came from the Supreme Being. ।। 1 ।।

· · · ✦ · · ·

The Lord, who said in the tenth chapter that among trees he is the ashvattha, here shows the same ashvattha as a symbol of worldly existence.

· · · ✦ · · ·

Ashvattha — the peepal tree; commentators say it stands as a symbol of the ever-changing world, in the sense of "that which does not last till tomorrow."
Urdhvamula — with roots above, that is, the Supreme Being who is the original source of creation.
Adhahshakha — with branches spread downward, that is, the creation spread out in the world.
Chhandamsi — the Vedas; commentators think that as leaves protect a tree, the Vedas run worldly life.

adhas cordhvam prasrtas tasya sakha
guna-pravrddha visaya-pravalah
adhas ca mulany anusantatani
karmanubandhini manusya-loke
"Its branches, nourished by the qualities, spread below and above; sense objects are its tender shoots. Its roots, which bind through action, extend downward as well in the world of men." Like new leaves sprouting, sense objects attract the mind; each action puts down a new root and ties a person more to this world. ।। 2 ।।

· · · ✦ · · ·

This is the tree-picture of what was said in the fourteenth chapter, that the qualities sattva, rajas, and tamas bind the soul by attachment to happiness, action, and negligence.

· · · ✦ · · ·

Vishayapravalas — sense objects as tender shoots; things that look new and attract.
Karmanubandhi mulas — roots that entwine through the fruits of action and through impressions.

నాంతో న చాదిర్న చ సంప్రతిష్ఠా ।
అశ్వత్థమేనం సువిరూఢమూలమ్
అసంగశస్త్రేణ ధృఢేన ఛిత్త్వా ।। 3 ।।
ఈ వృక్షము యొక్క నిజ స్వరూపము ఈ జగత్తులో గ్రహింపబడదు; దాని యొక్క మొదలు, చివర, లేదా సనాతన అస్తిత్వము కూడా అర్థం కావు. కానీ, ఈ యొక్క లోతైన వేర్లు కల అశ్వత్థ వృక్షమును అనాసక్తి/వైరాగ్యమనే బలమైన గొడ్డలిచే ఖండించివేయాలి. ఆ తరువాత ఆ వృక్షము యొక్క మొదలు వెతకాలి; అదియే ఆ భగవంతుడు, ఆయన నుండే ఈ జగత్తు యొక్క ఉత్పత్తి సనాతన కాలం క్రితం సంభవించినది. ఆయనను ఆశ్రయించిన తరువాత మళ్ళీ మనం ఈ జగత్తు లోనికి రాము.
na rupam asyeha tathopalabhyate
nanto na cadir na ca sampratistha
asvattham enam su-virudha-mulam
asanga-sastrena drdhena chittva
"Its form is not perceived here as such, nor its end, nor its beginning, nor its foundation. Cutting this ashvattha, whose roots are very firmly grown, with the strong weapon of non-attachment;" this sentence is completed in the next verse. We cannot see the beginning or the end of worldly existence; that is why the Lord says to cut it not with arguments, but with the weapon called asanga, that is, non-attachment. ।। 3 ।।

· · · ✦ · · ·

The Lord, who said at the end of the fourth chapter to cut through doubt with the sword of knowledge and stand up, here says to cut the tree of worldly existence with the weapon of non-attachment.

· · · ✦ · · ·

Asanga — absence of clinging; it is the same practice of giving up attachment to results that has been taught since the third chapter.
Suvirudha mula — one with deeply rooted, firm roots.

The sentence that began in the previous verse is completed here: "After that, that state must be sought, reaching which they do not return again. "Those free from pride and delusion, who have conquered the fault of attachment, who always abide in the Self, whose desires have fully ceased, who are freed from the dualities known as pleasure and pain, and who are not deluded, reach that imperishable state." These are not new qualities; they are the essence of the qualities told so far gathered in one place. ।। 5 ।।

· · · ✦ · · ·

The qualities of knowledge such as absence of pride and steadiness in knowledge of the Self, spoken of in the thirteenth chapter, and those who see pleasure and pain alike, spoken of in the second, appear here as steps to the final goal.

· · · ✦ · · ·

Nirmanamohas — those without pride and delusion.
Jitasangadoshas — those who have conquered the fault of attachment.
Adhyatmanityas — those who always abide in knowledge of the Self spoken of in the eighth chapter.
Amudhas — those who do not fall into delusion, who have clear understanding.

··· In Real Life ···

As pride, our hold on others, and unnecessary desires lessen, the mind becomes lighter. A person who is not swept away whether happiness or sorrow comes also gives courage to those around him. A little effort every day toward such a mind is the path this verse shows.
